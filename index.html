Applied AI prototypes

Built to make the workflow inspectable · outcomes not yet validated
Local AI bridge required · prototype

AI Assignment Grading Assistant

Turns assignment requirements into a draft rubric for teacher review and locking. AI then prepares criterion-linked preliminary scoring; the teacher reviews, edits, and confirms the final grade. When enabled, submission text, assignment name, and rubric pass through a local bridge to the configured model provider; this is not a hosted grader.

API cost: author-reported small-scale DeepSeek test mean ~CNY 0.2 per assignment. Scope is not standardized; actual costs vary; not a quote, benchmark, or guarantee.

Teacher-side prototype · separate student page

Muzhi Livestock Teaching Assistant

Turns an original or authorized teaching-case image, with an optional learning focus, into an editable question draft. The image and prompt/context are sent to the configured DeepSeek API. A teacher checks the question, answer, and explanation before saving to the current browser's local question bank, which is not connected to the separate student practice page.

API cost: author-reported small-scale test mean ≤ CNY 0.10 per question for image recognition + generation. Scope is not standardized; actual costs vary; not a quote, benchmark, or guarantee; teacher review is excluded.

Read the public case →

Both problem statements are product hypotheses, not conclusions from user interviews. Accuracy, teacher time savings, classroom adoption, and learning outcomes have not been validated. The livestock prototype is for teaching practice—not animal diagnosis or treatment guidance.

    A practitioner-researcher

    Working at the intersection of AI, decision-making, technology, and institutions. The trajectory bends from a language and cross-cultural background into information systems, financial systems, government digital systems, AI/Data, teaching, and now research.

    Humanities → Information Systems → Financial Systems
    → Digital Government → AI / Data → Product
    → Human-AI Decision Making → AI Governance / Philosophy of Technology

    Practice

    From N-PORT regulatory reporting at State Street to three provincial big-data platforms under "Digital Yunnan," from on-chain trading systems at NX Lab to AI grading systems in development — practice has always been about making decision-grade data legible and trustworthy.

    Research

    The doctoral programme traces a single recursive structure: human–AI reliance → explanation → control → governance. Four themes organise the work: Human–AI Decision Making, AI Governance, Sociotechnical Systems, and Philosophy of Technology.

    Methods

    Computational social science meets philosophical analysis. Product management meets field research. The methods are hybrid because the questions sit between disciplines — between a philosopher's question and a product manager's edge case.

    Direction

    Applying to doctoral programs in Philosophy of Technology / Science & Technology Studies (STS). The goal is not to leave practice behind, but to give it a longer arc — from closing one edge case at a time to understanding the grammar that connects them all.
